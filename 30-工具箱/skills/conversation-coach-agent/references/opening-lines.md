# Opening Lines — Conversation Starters by Type

## Why Opening Lines Matter

The first 15 seconds of a high-stakes conversation sets the tone
for everything that follows. A weak opening creates defensiveness
or signals insecurity. A strong opening creates psychological safety
and orients the conversation toward the outcome you want.

---

## JOB INTERVIEW OPENERS

### "Tell me about yourself" (The Most Common, Least Prepared For)

**Bad versions:**
- "Well, I graduated from [school] in [year] and then I worked at..."
  (chronological recitation — no narrative, no hook)
- "I've been in marketing for 12 years and I've done a lot of different things..."
  (vague, doesn't differentiate)

**Strong structure:**
[Where I've been → What I've built → Why THIS role]

**Example:**
"I've spent the last six years building growth teams at B2B SaaS companies —
most recently at [company] where I led the team that grew ARR from $2M to $12M.
I'm at a point where I want to apply that in a larger market, and what
drew me to [this company] specifically was [genuine specific reason].
I think there's a real match between what I've built and what you're
trying to do here."

**Rule:** End with why THIS role, not just "and that's why I'm interested
in a new opportunity." Generic endings waste the strong start.

---

### Opening Before the Interviewer Speaks
When you have the chance to set the tone before being asked anything:
"I'm excited to be here — I've been following [company] for a while and
this role in particular feels like a strong match for where I am right now.
Looking forward to the conversation."

Brief, specific, confident. Not "thank you so much for having me."

## SALARY NEGOTIATION OPENERS

### For a New Job Offer
**Never lead with the ask.** Lead with enthusiasm first.

"I'm really excited about this opportunity — I was impressed by
[specific thing] during the process, and I'm very motivated to
join the team. I did want to talk about the compensation package
before I accept. Is this a good time?"

**Then let them respond. Don't fill the silence.**

### For a Raise at Current Job

**Schedule a specific meeting** (don't ambush).
Request subject: "I'd like to find 20 minutes to talk about my role
and compensation." Don't reveal the full agenda — you want them to be
present and open, not pre-defensive.

**Opening in the meeting:**
"I wanted to find time for this conversation because I'm really
invested in the work here — and I want to make sure we're aligned
on my role and what I'm contributing. Can we talk about compensation?"

**Framing principle:** "I'm here because I want to STAY — let's make
sure this works for both of us."

## DIFFICULT CONVERSATION WITH MANAGER OPENERS

**The intent-first rule:** The first sentence should name WHY
you're having the conversation.

**Weak openers:**
- "I need to talk to you about something." (Vague, creates dread)
- "Do you have a minute?" (Always says yes, then you're unprepared)
- "I've been frustrated about..." (Leads with emotion, creates defensiveness)

**Strong openers:**
- "I wanted to find time to talk because I want to make sure we're
  working well together — there's something I'd like to get your
  thoughts on."
- "I care about doing good work here, and I want to be direct with
  you about something that's been affecting my work."
- "I've been thinking about how to bring this up in the right way
  because I want it to be a productive conversation — can I share
  something with you?"

---

## DIFFICULT PERSONAL CONVERSATION OPENERS

**The golden opener formula:**
[I'm raising this because I care] + [I'm not attacking you] + [I want to resolve this]

**Examples:**
- "I've been putting off this conversation because I wasn't sure how to
  start — but I think not saying it is starting to hurt us more than
  saying it. Can we talk about [topic]?"
- "I want to start by saying I love you / value our friendship — and
  that's exactly why I need to be honest about something."
- "I've been sitting with this for a while and I think it's better
  that I say it than keep letting it build. Can I share something
  with you?"

**What all strong openers have in common:**
- They name the relationship as the reason for the conversation
- They signal non-attack before the content lands
- They ask for permission ("can we talk?") — which gives the
  other person agency and reduces defensiveness

## MEDICAL APPOINTMENT OPENERS

### First Appointment for a New/Concerning Issue

"The main thing I want to talk about today is [symptom/concern].
It started [timeframe], it happens [frequency], and the part that
concerns me most is [specific impact on daily life]."

Lead with the most important thing FIRST — doctors often run over
time and you don't want to get to the important thing at the end.

### Pushing for More Than "Wait and See"

"I want to understand what we're waiting to see — what would
need to happen for us to take further action? And is there
anything I should be watching for in the meantime?"

### Seeking a Second Opinion

"I've been thinking about getting a second opinion — not because
I doubt your judgment, but because I want to make sure I've
explored everything available. Could you help me think through
what that process would look like?"

## PRESENTATION OPENERS

### The Problem-First Opening (Most Effective)

Don't start with your name, title, or agenda. Start with the problem.

**Weak:** "Hi everyone, I'm [name] from [team]. Today I'm going to
present our findings on [topic]. Here's the agenda..."

**Strong:** "We're leaving [specific amount] on the table every quarter —
and I think we know how to fix it. Let me show you what we found."

**Or:** "Most companies in our position are [thing]. We're not —
and that gap is costing us [specific thing]. Here's what I'm proposing."

### The Question Opening
"How many of you have encountered [problem]?"
[Pause for response]
"Right. That's what we're here to solve today."

### The Story Opening (For Pitches or Emotional Buy-In)
"Six months ago, a customer told me [story that represents the problem].
That conversation is why I'm here today."

---

## Universal Strong-Opener Elements

Any opener for a high-stakes conversation benefits from:

1. **Specificity** — a specific fact, number, or observation beats
   any vague opener
2. **Forward-lean** — positioned as moving toward something, not
   away from something
3. **Clarity of purpose** — the other person should know within
   30 seconds why you're here and what you want
4. **Non-combative framing** — for interpersonal conversations,
   signal collaboration before content
5. **Confidence in delivery** — even a perfect opener delivered
   with a shaky voice signals insecurity. Slow down, make eye
   contact (or in phone: pause before speaking), speak from
   your chest not your throat.
