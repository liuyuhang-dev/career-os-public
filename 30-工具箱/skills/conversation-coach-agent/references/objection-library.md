# Objection Library — Common Pushbacks & How to Handle Them

## How to Use

For each conversation type, these are the most common pushbacks the user
will face. For each one:
1. Show them the likely objection
2. Coach them on the response
3. Practice that specific moment in roleplay

The format for each: **Objection → Why they say it → Response framework → Example language**

---

## 💼 JOB INTERVIEW OBJECTIONS

### "Tell me about your greatest weakness"
**Why they ask:** To see self-awareness and growth mindset
**Bad answers:** "I work too hard" / "I'm a perfectionist" (eye-roll)
**Framework:** Real weakness + what you've learned/done about it + progress
**Example:** "I used to struggle with [real thing]. I noticed it when [situation].
I've worked on it by [specific action] and now [progress/result]."

---

### "Why do you want to leave your current job?"
**Why they ask:** Red flags check — are you running from something?
**Bad answers:** "My boss is terrible" / anything negative about current employer
**Framework:** Running TOWARD, not away. Forward-looking.
**Example:** "I've learned a lot at [current company] and I'm proud of what
I've built there. I'm at a point where I'm looking for [specific growth/
challenge/type of work] — and this role is exactly that."

---

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
**Why they ask:** Retention risk, ambition check, alignment
**Framework:** Ambition within realistic scope of the company
**Example:** "I'd like to have grown significantly in [area], and contributed
to [something meaningful at company level]. I'm genuinely here to build
something — not just pass through."

---

### "We have other strong candidates"
**Why they say it:** True, or negotiating tactic, or both
**Framework:** Acknowledge + reaffirm your value without desperation
**Example:** "I'd expect you to — this sounds like a great role. What I can
tell you is [specific value I bring]. I hope I've shown that today."

---

### "Your experience doesn't perfectly match what we're looking for"
**Framework:** Bridge the gap — what's transferable, what you'll learn fast
**Example:** "I hear that — I don't have direct [X] experience. What I do
have is [related thing]. I've also learned [adjacent skill] quickly before
when [example]. I'm confident the gap is smaller than it might look on paper."

## 💰 SALARY NEGOTIATION OBJECTIONS

### "That's outside our budget / range"
**Why they say it:** True, OR the standard first response to any negotiation
**Framework:** Explore, don't cave. "Help me understand the constraints."
**Example:** "I appreciate you being direct. Can you help me understand what
the range looks like? I want to find a path that works for both of us."
**Follow-up if truly capped:** "If base is fixed, is there flexibility on
[bonus / equity / start date / review timing / title]?"

---

### "We're offering the same as your current salary"
**Framework:** Current salary is a separate data point — market value is what matters
**Example:** "I appreciate the offer. I've been looking at market data for this
role, and [number] is more in line with what comparable roles are offering.
I'd like to discuss whether there's flexibility there."

---

### "This is our standard offer / we don't negotiate"
**Why they say it:** Usually not true. HR says this to avoid negotiating with
everyone, but most individuals CAN negotiate.
**Framework:** Acknowledge + gently push
**Example:** "I understand there's a standard structure. Given [my specific
experience / the scope of this role / my situation], I was hoping to discuss
whether there's any flexibility. Even [small concession] would mean a lot."

---

### "We'll revisit compensation in 6 months at your review"
**Framework:** Get it in writing or get more now — reviews are not guaranteed
**Example:** "I'm glad to hear that. Could we put something in writing about
the target range at that review? I want to make sure we're aligned on
what success looks like for that conversation."

---

### "I need to check with [HR / leadership] and get back to you"
**Framework:** This is normal — don't read it as a no
**Example:** "Of course — I appreciate you checking. Is there a timeline
for that? I have a few other things I'm considering, so it helps me to
have a sense of when to expect to hear back."

## 😤 DIFFICULT CONVERSATION WITH MANAGER OBJECTIONS

### "I don't think that's how it happened"
**Framework:** Acknowledge their perception + hold your experience
**Example:** "I hear that you see it differently. I can only speak to how
it landed for me — and I want to make sure we understand each other."

---

### "Everyone has to deal with this / that's just how it works here"
**Framework:** Normalize ≠ acceptable. Hold the impact without fighting the system.
**Example:** "I understand that's been the pattern. What I'm saying is that
for me, the impact has been [specific], and I wanted to bring it to you
directly because I think we can work together better."

---

### "I'm surprised you feel this way — you never brought it up before"
**Framework:** Own it, move forward
**Example:** "You're right — I should have raised this sooner. I'm raising
it now because I want to address it before it affects our work further."

---

### "Are you happy here?"
**Framework:** This is a loaded question. Answer strategically.
**Example:** "I am — which is why I'm bringing this up directly instead of
just disengaging. I want to make this work."

---

### "I'll take that into consideration" (non-committal deflection)
**Framework:** Pin down a next step
**Example:** "I appreciate that. Can we schedule a follow-up to talk about
this again in [specific time]? I want to make sure we've resolved this."

---

## 💔 DIFFICULT PERSONAL CONVERSATION OBJECTIONS

### "You're being too sensitive"
**Framework:** Don't defend your feelings — hold them
**Example:** "Maybe — but this is how I'm experiencing it, and I wanted to
share that with you rather than let it build. That's not me attacking you —
that's me being honest with someone I care about."

---

### "I can't believe you feel this way after everything I've done"
**Framework:** Acknowledge their effort + hold your truth
**Example:** "I know you do a lot. I'm not dismissing that. What I'm
saying is [specific thing] has been affecting me, and I need us to talk
about it."

---

### "This is coming out of nowhere"
**Framework:** It's not out of nowhere for YOU — explain
**Example:** "I can see why it might feel that way — I haven't said it
directly before. But this has been building for me for a while. I'm
saying it now because I care about where we're headed."

---

### "So you're saying I'm a bad [partner / friend / parent]?"
**Framework:** This is catastrophizing — redirect
**Example:** "No — I'm saying [specific behavior] has been having
[specific impact] on me. That's not the same as a judgment of who you are."

---

### "Fine, let's talk about what I need then" (turning it around)
**Framework:** Don't abandon your point — complete the exchange
**Example:** "I genuinely want to hear that too. Can we finish with what
I'm sharing first, and then I want to make sure I understand your perspective?"

---

## 🏥 MEDICAL APPOINTMENT OBJECTIONS

### "Let's just wait and see"
**Framework:** Ask what the waiting is for / what would change the recommendation
**Example:** "I understand. What would need to happen — or what would we
be watching for — to decide it's time to act? And is there anything I
should be doing in the meantime?"

---

### "That's probably just stress / anxiety"
**Framework:** Validate, but don't let it close the conversation
**Example:** "I'm sure stress plays a role. Even so, I'd like to make
sure we've ruled out other causes. What would that process look like?"

---

### "The test results are within normal range"
**Framework:** "Normal range" ≠ normal for you
**Example:** "I understand they're technically within range. I'm still
experiencing [symptoms] that are significantly affecting my [daily life].
Is there anything else we should look at, or is there a specialist who
might have more insight?"

---

### "There isn't much more we can do"
**Framework:** Ask explicitly about other options
**Example:** "I appreciate your honesty. Are there specialists you'd
recommend I see? Or are there newer treatments or trials you're aware of?
I want to make sure I've explored everything available."
