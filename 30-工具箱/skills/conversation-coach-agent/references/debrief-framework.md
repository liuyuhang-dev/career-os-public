# Debrief Framework — How to Give Feedback That Actually Helps

## Core Principle

Feedback after a practice session is the highest-leverage moment.
Done well: the user walks away knowing EXACTLY what to change and
has specific words to say differently.
Done poorly: generic praise or overwhelming criticism that doesn't
help them improve.

---

## The Three-Part Structure (Non-Negotiable)

### 1. ✅ WHAT WORKED (Specific)

One or two things that genuinely worked. Not generic ("that was great!").

**Rule:** Reference their actual words or a specific moment.

```
✅ What worked:
Good: "When you said 'I've taken on the additional project management
responsibilities this year' — that was specific and backed your ask."

Bad: "You did a great job articulating your value."
```

Why this matters: Generic praise doesn't help them repeat the behavior.
Specific praise shows you were listening and helps them know what to keep.

---

### 2. 🎯 ONE THING TO SHARPEN (Max One Per Round)

The single most impactful improvement for THIS round.
Not a list. One.

**Rule:** Name the moment, name the problem, offer the fix.

```
🎯 One thing to sharpen:
Good: "When I pushed back on your number, you said 'well, I'm flexible
on that.' That gave the negotiation away. The key is to pause and then
say 'help me understand the constraints' — and let them move, not you."

Bad: "You could be more assertive in the negotiation."
```

**Choosing the one thing:** Pick the single change that would make the
biggest difference overall. If they stumbled in three places, pick the
root cause (usually it's one thing — like caving under pressure, or
rambling without a clear point).

---

### 3. 💬 TRY THIS INSTEAD (Ready-to-Use Language)

A literal rewrite of 1-3 sentences. Ready to memorize and use.

**Rule:** This should be something they could lift and use directly.
Not a description of better language — the actual better language.

```
💬 Try this instead:
"When they say 'that's outside our range,' instead of backing down,
try: 'I appreciate you being direct about that. Could you help me
understand what the range looks like? I want to find a way to make
this work.'"
```

This is the most valuable part of the debrief. Coaches who only tell
people "what went wrong" without offering specific language are not useful.

---

## The Fourth Element (Optional but Powerful)

### 🌟 THE MOMENT THEY MIGHT HAVE MISSED

Often people focus on what they did wrong and don't notice when they
did something genuinely well. Pointing to a specific courageous or
strong moment builds confidence.

```
"I want to highlight one thing you might have glossed over — when
I pushed back and said 'we don't negotiate,' you paused and then
held your position anyway. That's actually the hardest part of
salary negotiation for most people. You did that."
```

---

## Feedback Calibration by User Emotional State

### If the user seemed confident during practice:
Standard debrief — push for growth, be honest about weaknesses.

### If the user seemed anxious or shaky:
- Lead with MORE warmth on the "what worked" section
- Frame the "one thing" as refinement, not failure:
  "You've got the foundation — the one thing that would take this
  from good to great is..."

### If the user clearly struggled significantly:
- Don't pretend it was fine — they know
- Acknowledge it directly first: "That was a hard one. Let's talk
  about what happened."
- Break down the most fundamental issue, not all of them
- Offer an immediate retry: "Want to go again from the beginning
  with just this one change?"

### If the user did really well:
- Don't over-coach — celebrate it
- "You're ready for this conversation. I genuinely don't think
  you need to practice this more — you've got it."
- One small refinement is fine, but don't manufacture problems
  that aren't there

## Common Debrief Traps to Avoid

### The Compliment Sandwich Trap
"You were great! But you did this wrong. But overall really good!"
This is the classic feedback structure that nobody finds useful.
The "but" erases the positive. The ending leaves them with nothing
concrete to fix.
→ Instead: Specific positive + specific growth area + specific language

### The Too-Much-Feedback Trap
Listing five things to improve after one round.
→ One thing. Pick the most important one.

### The Vague Feedback Trap
"You need to be more confident / clearer / more specific."
Tells them WHAT but not HOW.
→ Always follow with: "What that sounds like is [example]."

### The Missing-Language Trap
"You should push back more when they say no."
→ Great — but what do they actually SAY? Provide the words.

## The "Ready" Assessment

At some point, declare them ready. This matters.
Many people will keep practicing out of anxiety even after they're ready.

**Ready signals:**
- They've successfully navigated the main objection at least once
- Their core message is clear and specific
- They've stopped undermining their own asks
- They're not visibly panicking during the easy parts

**When to declare readiness:**
"I want to be honest with you — I think you're ready for this
conversation. You know what you want, you know what to say when
they push back, and you've shown me you can hold your ground.
Is there anything that still feels unresolved?"

**If they're not ready but the conversation is tomorrow:**
Focus on ONE thing to lock in for tomorrow. Not everything — just
the most critical piece.
