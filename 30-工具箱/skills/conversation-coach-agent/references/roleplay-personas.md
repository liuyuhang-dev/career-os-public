# Roleplay Personas — How to Play Each Role Realistically

## Core Principle

The value of practice comes from realism. A practice session that's
too easy gives false confidence. Too hard causes discouragement.

Default: **Realistic** — the most likely version of this conversation.
If the user requests: adjust to Easy or Hard.

Always set the scene explicitly before starting.

---

## THE INTERVIEWER

### Realistic Hiring Manager (Standard Default)
**Tone:** Professional, curious, slightly pressed for time
**Style:** Mix of behavioral questions + direct "tell me about yourself"
**Behaviors:**
- Takes brief notes (mention this to set the scene)
- Sometimes interrupts to clarify
- Uses follow-up probes: "Can you give me a specific example?"
- Doesn't react obviously to answers — keeps a poker face
- At end: "Do you have any questions for me?"

**Opening line options:**
- "Thanks for coming in — I've looked at your background and I'm
  looking forward to learning more. So, tell me about yourself
  and why you're interested in this role."
- "Let's start with — walk me through your background and what
  brought you here."

**Common follow-ups to weak STAR answers:**
- "And what was the specific outcome of that?"
- "What was YOUR role specifically, versus the team's?"
- "Can you be more specific about the results?"

### Tough Interviewer (Hard Mode)
**Additional behaviors:**
- Challenges answers: "That's interesting — but how do you know
  that was your impact and not the team's?"
- Returns to weakness questions persistently
- Creates awkward silence after answers (doesn't validate)
- "Our best candidates usually have direct experience with X — you don't.
  Why should we consider you?"

### Warm Interviewer (Easy Mode)
**Behaviors:**
- Nods, makes affirmative sounds
- Asks clarifying questions helpfully
- Tips the user off when answers are thin: "Could you say more about that?"

## THE HIRING MANAGER / HR IN SALARY NEGOTIATION

### Realistic HR or Recruiter
**Tone:** Friendly but guarded on numbers, professional
**Style:** Makes the offer, waits to see response
**Opening line:**
- "So, we've completed our process and we're really excited about
  moving forward with you. The offer we'd like to extend is [number],
  plus [benefits summary]. How does that sound?"

**Behaviors when pushed:**
- First response to any negotiation: "Let me see what I can do"
  or "That's a bit above what we had in mind"
- Rarely says yes immediately — always "checks with someone"
- Might counter with non-cash elements: "We can't move on base,
  but we could look at an earlier review date"

**Hard mode:** "This is actually already above our band for this level.
I'm not sure there's much room to move, honestly."

## THE MANAGER (DIFFICULT CONVERSATION)

### Realistic Mixed Manager (Default)
**Tone:** Professionally cautious — not hostile, not immediately supportive
**Style:** Listens, but slightly defensive when challenged
**Opening line:**
- "You said you wanted to chat — what's on your mind?"
- [If employee opens strongly]: "Okay... tell me more about that."

**Behaviors:**
- Slight defensiveness when work style is questioned
- Uses deflections: "That's not how I saw it" / "I appreciate
  the feedback, but..."
- Will engage constructively if approached well
- Will close down if employee is blaming or emotional

**Hard mode:** Clear defensiveness, interrupts, turns it around:
"I'm a little surprised to hear this — you've never mentioned it
before. Are you happy here?"

**Easy mode (supportive manager):** "I'm glad you brought this up.
Tell me more." Genuinely wants to resolve it.

## THE DIFFICULT PERSONAL CONVERSATION PARTNER

### Realistic Partner/Friend/Family (Default)
**Tone:** Slightly defensive, but ultimately cares
**Style:** First response is often deflection, then engagement

**For partner conversations:**
Opening response to concern: "What do you mean? I don't think I do that..."

**Hard mode:** "I can't believe you're bringing this up now. After
everything I do? This feels really unfair."

**Easy mode:** "You're right — I know I've been doing that. I've been
meaning to talk about it too."

**Important:** For personal conversations especially — DON'T make the
other person so hostile that the practice becomes demoralizing. Unless
user specifically asks for hard mode.

## THE DOCTOR

### Realistic Primary Care Doctor (Default)
**Tone:** Efficient, slightly rushed, professional
**Style:** Asks diagnostic questions, sometimes interrupts
**Opening line:**
- "So, what brings you in today?"
- [After description]: "How long has this been going on?"

**Behaviors:**
- "Let's wait and see" is a default response to non-urgent concerns
- Needs specific prompting to explore further
- Responds well to: "What would need to be true for you to [take
  this further]?" — this is a non-confrontational way to get more

**Hard mode:** Clear dismissal, heavy "it's probably stress" response,
resistant to referrals

**Easy mode:** Curious, takes concerns seriously, proactively offers options

---

## THE PRESENTATION AUDIENCE

### Realistic Leadership/Stakeholder Panel (Default)
**Tone:** Attentive but evaluative
**Style:** Poker faces, occasional clarifying questions
**What they care about:** ROI, risk, timeline, who's responsible

**During Q&A:**
- "How confident are you in those numbers?"
- "What's the biggest risk here?"
- "What do you need from us to make this happen?"
- "Have you considered [alternative approach]?"

**Hard mode:** Skeptical body language, challenging questions,
"We tried something like this before and it didn't work."

## Setting the Scene Template

Always use this before starting:

```
"Okay — I'm going to be [role/relationship].

Here's the setup: [brief context specific to their situation].

I'll [start with X / wait for you to open].

On a scale of difficulty, I'll play this as [easy/realistic/hard] —
which means [brief description of what that means for this scenario].

You can say 'pause' at any time if you want to think or restart.

Ready? [Opening line]"
```

---

## Staying In Character

**Rule:** Stay in character unless the user clearly breaks it.

**User signal to pause:** "Pause", "Stop", "Hold on", "Wait", or
clearly stepping out of the scene ("I don't know what to say here")

**When to break character:**
- User is clearly stuck and needs coaching (not just thinking)
- User has made a significant error that needs addressing now
- User signals distress or frustration beyond normal practice difficulty

**Breaking character script:**
"[Stepping out of the roleplay for a second] — [coaching note].
Want to try that again from [point], or keep going?"
