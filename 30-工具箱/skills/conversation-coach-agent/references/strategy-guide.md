# Strategy Guide — Preparation Framework

## The Three Foundations

Every high-stakes conversation needs these three things clear:

### 1. Your Goal
Not "I want this to go well" — that's an outcome, not a goal.

**Format:** "By the end of this conversation, the other person will
[understand / agree to / see / take action on] [specific thing]."

**Examples:**
- "By the end, my manager will know I'm serious about a promotion and
  we'll have agreed on a timeline."
- "By the end, the interviewer will see me as the strongest candidate
  for this specific role."
- "By the end, my partner will understand how this pattern has been
  affecting me, and we'll have agreed to try something different."

### 2. Your Core Message (30 seconds)
If the user could only say one thing, what would it be?

**Format:** "[Context] + [What I want/need] + [Why it matters]"

Drill: "Can you say that in one sentence?" — if they can't,
the message isn't clear yet.

### 3. Your Emotional Foundation
High-stakes conversations require emotional regulation. Establish:
- What's the worst realistic thing that could happen? (Name it — it's
  less scary when named)
- Is the user's self-worth tied to the outcome? (If yes, address this:
  "Your value isn't on the table here — just one conversation")
- What does the user want to feel DURING the conversation?
  (Calm, clear, and confident beats "winning")

## Strategy by Conversation Type

### 💼 Job Interview Strategy

**Pre-interview clarity:**
- The "Why I want this role" story (authentic, specific, not generic)
- 3 strength stories in STAR format — ready to deploy for any behavioral Q
- The answer to "tell me about yourself" (90 seconds, specific, ends with
  why THIS role)
- The weakness answer that shows self-awareness without torpedoing you

**Power moves:**
- Ask good questions at the end — shows engagement, not desperation
- Name the role specifically when talking about it ("in this VP Marketing
  role..." not "if I get this job...")
- Mirror the company's language and values back to them

**Mindset:** "I'm also evaluating them." This reduces panic.

### 💰 Salary Negotiation Strategy

**The core principle:** The first person to name a specific number
is in a weaker position. Try to get them to go first. If you must
go first — go higher than your target.

**The sequence:**
1. Express genuine enthusiasm first (don't lead with the ask)
2. Make the ask with a specific number (not a range — ranges anchor to
   the lower number)
3. Silence. Let them respond. Don't fill the silence.
4. If they push back: "I hear you — help me understand the constraints.
   Is there flexibility on [base / bonus / equity / start date]?"

**The anchoring principle:** Your first number matters more than
your negotiating. Start higher than your target.

**Salary negotiation script skeleton:**
"I'm really excited about this opportunity — I've been impressed by
[specific thing]. I was hoping we could talk about the compensation.
Based on my experience with [specific things], I was thinking
[number]. Is there flexibility there?"

**Then: silence.**

**When they say "that's outside our range":**
"I appreciate you being direct about that. Can you help me understand
what the range looks like? I want to find a way to make this work."

**Your BATNA matters:** If you have another offer or solid current
position — you don't NEED this offer. That changes how you negotiate.

### 😤 Difficult Conversation with Manager Strategy

**The opening matters most.** The first 30 seconds sets the tone.

**Bad opening:** "I need to talk to you about something that's been
bothering me." (Immediately defensive.)

**Good opening:** "I wanted to find time to talk because I care about
doing good work here, and I want to make sure we're on the same page
about [topic]."

**The non-blame frame:** Focus on impact, not intent.
- Instead of: "You micromanage me and it's undermining my work"
- Try: "When decisions get reviewed after I've already made them,
  it's been slowing down projects. I wanted to understand how to
  work better together on this."

**The ask:** Be specific. "I'd like to..." is better than vague
complaints. Have a concrete proposed solution.

**The SBI model (Situation-Behavior-Impact):**
"In [situation], when [behavior], I noticed [impact]."
This is factual, not accusatory.

### 💔 Difficult Personal Conversation Strategy

**Intent-first rule:** Start by naming WHY you're having this
conversation. Without this, people hear the content as an attack.

"I want to start by saying — I'm bringing this up because
[the relationship matters to me / I want us to work through this /
I care about you and want to be honest]."

**The core pattern:** FEEL → NEED → ASK
1. What I feel: "I've been feeling [emotion] when [situation]"
2. What I need: "What I need is [specific thing]"
3. What I'm asking: "What I'm asking for is [concrete ask]"

**Avoid:**
- "You always..." / "You never..." (exaggerations invite debate about
  the exaggeration, not the issue)
- "You make me feel..." (they can't control your feelings — own them)
- Bringing in every past grievance in one conversation

**Prepare for:**
- Defensiveness → "I hear that. I'm not trying to make you feel attacked.
  I'm sharing this because it matters to me."
- Minimizing → "I understand it might seem small. For me, it's been
  building up for a while."
- Turning it around → "I want to hear your perspective too — can we
  finish with my concern first and then I want to make sure I understand
  yours?"

### 🏥 Medical Appointment Strategy

**The 2-minute symptom summary:**
- When did this start?
- How often does it happen?
- What does it feel like (specific, not just "bad")?
- What makes it better/worse?
- What have you tried?
- How is it affecting your daily life? (This matters — it communicates
  severity better than medical descriptions)

**The ask must be specific:** "I'd like to [get a referral to a
specialist / order an MRI / try a different medication / understand
why the current treatment isn't working]."

**When the doctor dismisses:**
"I understand you might see this differently. It's been significantly
affecting my [work/sleep/daily activities]. I want to make sure we've
considered all options. What would need to be true for you to [order
the test / refer me to a specialist]?"

**Second opinion phrase:**
"I've been thinking about getting a second opinion on this. Not because
I doubt you — I just want to make sure I've explored everything. Could
you recommend someone, or is that something I'd arrange separately?"

### 📊 Presentation Strategy

**The "so what" test:** For every slide or section, ask: "So what?"
If you can't answer that in one sentence — cut or reframe.

**The audience alignment:** What does THIS audience care about most?
Frame everything in their terms, not yours.

**The narrative structure:**
1. Start with the problem (or opportunity) — not with your solution
2. Show you understand the stakes (for THEM)
3. Present your approach/solution
4. Show evidence / expected outcomes
5. Clear call to action (what you need from them, specifically)

**Managing nerves:**
- Slow down — nerves make people speak faster, which makes audiences
  uncomfortable and makes speakers seem anxious
- Pause deliberately — silence reads as confidence, not weakness
- Know your first sentence by heart — confidence builds from the opening

**The Q&A:**
"That's a great question" — pause, then answer. Don't rush Q&A.
If you don't know: "I don't have that data in front of me — let me
follow up with you directly."
